# Building the question set

The question set is the whole instrument. Everything else is recording and
arithmetic. Get this wrong and the audit measures nothing.

The rule: **write what a customer would actually type or say, not what an SEO tool
would call a keyword.** People talk to assistants in full sentences with
constraints attached. "plumber rochester" is a search box habit. "My water heater
is leaking, who can come out today in Rochester" is how someone asks an assistant.

Thirty questions is the working number. Enough to be stable across a re-run, few
enough to finish in an afternoon.

---

## The seven question types

Cover all seven. The mix shifts by vertical, but skipping a type creates a blind
spot, and the blind spots are usually where the money is.

### 1. Bare category and place — about 5 questions
The headline test. If a business loses here it loses the widest funnel.

- "Who is the best {category} in {city}?"
- "Top rated {category} near {neighborhood}"
- "I need a {category} in {city}, who should I call?"

### 2. Urgent, job-shaped — about 6 questions
Highest intent and usually highest margin. This is where a business with real
availability should win and frequently does not, because availability is nowhere
a machine can read.

- "{Emergency symptom}, who can come out today in {city}?"
- "Is there a {category} open right now near {city}?"
- "Weekend / after hours {category} {city}"

### 3. Constraint-filtered — about 6 questions
The attribute test. Each question isolates one filter an assistant applies before
a human ever sees a name: service line, hours, credential, capacity, warranty,
service area, payment.

- "{Category} in {city} that works on {specific system or brand}"
- "{Category} near {city} that is licensed and insured"
- "{Category} that serves {adjacent town}"
- "{Category} in {city} that offers {financing / warranty / emergency service}"

A miss here is the most actionable finding in the whole audit, because it usually
means the business genuinely offers the thing and simply never published it in a
form anything can read.

### 4. Price-anchored — about 3 questions
Almost every SMB refuses to publish pricing. Assistants reward whoever does, even
with a starting range. This produces a reliably uncomfortable and reliably useful
finding.

- "How much does {common job} cost in {city}?"
- "Cheapest {category} near {city}"
- "Average price for {job} {region}"

### 5. Comparison — about 3 questions
Tests whether the business makes the consideration set at all.

- "{Competitor A} vs {Competitor B}, who is better for {job}?"
- "Who are the top three {category} businesses in {city}?"

Name real local competitors. If the business never appears in a comparison it did
not enter, that is the finding.

### 6. Branded and verification — about 4 questions
The only questions where the business is named up front. Tests what is *said*
about it rather than whether it is found. This section produces the accuracy
number and is usually the part that makes an owner sit up.

- "Is {business} any good?"
- "Is {business} licensed and insured?"
- "What are {business}'s hours?"
- "Does {business} do {service they actually do}?"

Verify every answer against ground truth before recording it. An assistant
confidently stating wrong hours is a finding, not a footnote.

### 7. Advice-shaped — about 3 questions
No business named, no explicit request for one. Tests whether the business surfaces
inside guidance, which is where a growing share of these conversations actually
start.

- "My {system} is {symptom}, what should I do?"
- "What should I know before replacing a {thing}?"
- "How do I choose a good {category}?"

## Worked example: residential plumbing, Rochester NY

Abbreviated. A real set runs to 30.

| # | Type | Question |
|---|------|----------|
| 1 | Category | Who is the best plumber in Rochester NY? |
| 2 | Category | Top rated plumbers near Irondequoit |
| 3 | Urgent | My pipe burst, who can come out today in Rochester? |
| 4 | Urgent | Is there an emergency plumber open right now near Rochester? |
| 5 | Urgent | Weekend plumber Rochester NY |
| 6 | Constraint | Plumber in Rochester that works on tankless water heaters |
| 7 | Constraint | Licensed and insured plumber near Rochester |
| 8 | Constraint | Plumber that serves Webster and Penfield |
| 9 | Price | How much does a water heater replacement cost in Rochester? |
| 10 | Price | Cheapest drain cleaning near Rochester |
| 11 | Comparison | Who are the top three plumbing companies in Rochester? |
| 12 | Branded | Is {business} licensed and insured? |
| 13 | Branded | What are {business}'s hours? |
| 14 | Branded | Does {business} do tankless installs? |
| 15 | Advice | My water heater is leaking from the bottom, what should I do? |

## Adapting to other verticals

The types do not change. The contents do.

- **Dental, medical, legal:** credentials, insurance accepted and new-patient
  availability dominate the constraint questions. Price questions become
  "does {practice} take {insurer}."
- **Contractors and trades:** licensing, bonding, lead time and service radius.
  Comparison questions matter more because the purchase is considered.
- **Retail and e-commerce:** stock, shipping speed, returns and product attributes
  replace hours and service area. Price questions get more weight.
- **B2B, distribution, manufacturing:** minimum order, lead time, certifications,
  capacity and territory. Advice-shaped questions dominate, because B2B buyers
  research the problem long before they name a vendor.

## Rules that keep a re-run honest

1. **Freeze the set.** Once an audit runs, those exact 30 questions are the
   instrument for that business forever. Changing them destroys the comparison,
   which is the entire product.
2. **No brand terms in the unbranded questions.** If the business name appears in
   questions 1 through 11, the result is meaningless.
3. **Use real competitor names** in comparison questions, and keep them fixed too.
4. **Write questions before looking at the business's website.** Otherwise the set
   gets written around what the business already says well, and the audit flatters
   instead of measures.
